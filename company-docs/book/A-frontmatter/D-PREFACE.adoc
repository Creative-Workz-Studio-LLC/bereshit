=== Preamble

[.text-center]
_Purpose, scope, and authority of this document._

[[P-1]]
==== § P.1 — Purpose

This manual defines who {company-name} is, what we do, why we exist, and how we operate.
It is the foundational governance document for the Company — not a formality, but a covenant record of our identity, mission, values, and standards.

{company-name} builds technology for the Kingdom — technology that honors God, serves others, and carries eternal value.
That mission requires that every person who participates in this work understands what they are joining, what is expected, and why.
This manual provides that understanding.

[[P-2]]
==== § P.2 — Scope and Authority

This manual serves as the authoritative reference for the identity, mission, values, and operational principles of {company-name}.
All Board members, Leadership, Team Members, and Partners are expected to be familiar with its contents as they relate to their respective roles and responsibilities.

[IMPORTANT]
====
Upon approval by the Board of Directors, this manual becomes the governing standard by which the Company measures its decisions, evaluates its partnerships, and holds its leadership accountable.
====

[[P-4]]
==== § P.4 — A Written Covenant

The prophet Habakkuk was told to write the vision plainly — not because God enjoys paperwork, but because a vision that is not written down becomes a vision that drifts.
The people who read it should be able to run with it.
That is the standard we hold this manual to.

// =============================================================================
// PAGE 4 — PREAMBLE: A WRITTEN COVENANT
// =============================================================================

This document is a *covenant*, not a contract.
A contract binds parties through obligation and enforcement — if one side fails, the other seeks remedy.
A covenant binds parties through shared commitment and accountability — if one side struggles, the other comes alongside.
We chose the word carefully, because it reflects the kind of relationships this Company was built on.

This manual does not exist to create legal liability between {company-short} and its people.
It exists to establish a shared standard that everyone who is part of this work voluntarily holds themselves to, because the mission is worth getting right.

When the Board approves this manual, they are affirming commitment.
When Team Members read it, they are encountering promises.
When Partners reference it, they are holding us to our word.
We write it down because our word must be our bond.

[[P-5]]
==== § P.5 — To the Reader

Thank you for reading this.
This manual is substantial — that is by design.
The vision it documents and the commitments it carries deserve thorough articulation, and we would rather be clear and complete than leave room for misunderstanding.

If you are a Board member receiving this for the first time, you are not expected to absorb all of it at once.
Each Board member will receive a review guide identifying the articles most relevant to your role and area of oversight.
Start there.
The full document serves as your ongoing reference as questions arise and decisions require grounding.

[[P-5a]]
===== § P.5a — How This Manual Is Organized

This manual is divided into four Parts, each building on the one before it:

* *Part I — Identity* (Articles 1–2) establishes who we are: the founding vision, the legal entity, and the publishing house model that defines how we engage with creators.
* *Part II — Operations* (Articles 3–5) documents what we build and what we provide: the Bereshit technology stack, the services we offer, and the audiences we serve.
* *Part III — Business* (Articles 6–7) defines how we sustain ourselves: revenue, economic philosophy, and the core values that govern every decision.
* *Part IV — Direction* (Articles 8–11) answers where we are going: the mission and vision, the team and <<def-board,Board>> structure, the timeline from foundation through the Millennium, and a complete synthesis of the whole.

Supporting material — including a Document Map, Scripture Index, Decision Frameworks, and Anti-Patterns Catalog — is collected in four Appendices.

[[P-5b]]
===== § P.5b — How to Reference This Document

Every section in this manual is tagged with a citable identifier using the *§* symbol.
For example, *§ P.1* refers to the Purpose section of this Preface, and *§ 2.3* refers to the third section of Article 2.
These identifiers allow precise reference in Board resolutions, meeting minutes, and official correspondence.

Terms that appear in *bold* in the definitions table (<<P-3>>) carry their defined meaning wherever they appear.
When you see "the Board," "the Company," "Leadership," or "Partners" in this manual, those words mean exactly what § P.3 says they mean.

[[P-5c]]
===== § P.5c — What "Board Review Draft" Means

This version of the manual is a Board Review Draft.
It is submitted for review and feedback — it is not yet the governing standard described in <<P-2>>.
It becomes that upon formal Board approval.

During the review period, we encourage every Board member to raise questions, flag concerns, and propose changes.
The goal is not ratification — it is genuine engagement.
When the Board approves this manual, every member should be able to do so with full understanding and clear conviction.

[[P-6]]
==== § P.6 — What This Manual Does

This manual does six things.
It defines who we are — identity grounded in Scripture, not in market positioning.
It articulates why we exist and what we are actually pursuing.
It establishes the values that govern every decision we make.
It documents how we operate — how things actually work, not how we wish they worked.
It aligns everyone around the same picture, so that <<def-board,the Board>>, <<def-leadership,Leadership>>, <<def-team-members,Team Members>>, and <<def-partners,Partners>> are reading from the same page.
And it creates accountability — not surveillance, but a standard everyone agreed to and can point back to when decisions need grounding.

[[P-7]]
==== § P.7 — What This Manual Does Not Do

This manual does not replace Scripture.
The Bible is the final authority for {company-name}, and this document derives from Scripture — it does not supersede it.
Where this manual and Scripture appear to conflict, Scripture governs.

This manual does not substitute for prayer or the leading of the Holy Spirit.
No governance document replaces discernment, and the people of this <<def-company,Company>> are expected to seek God in every decision, not simply consult a manual.

This manual does not provide rigid rules.
The principles documented here are constant; their application requires wisdom.
This is a guide, not a cage.
<<def-leadership,Leadership>> and <<def-team-members,Team Members>> are entrusted with the judgment to apply these principles faithfully in situations this manual cannot anticipate.

Finally, this manual is not static.
We learn, we grow, we correct.
The amendment procedures in Part IV exist because this document must evolve as God reveals more and as the <<def-company,Company>> matures.
What does not change is the foundation — the identity, mission, and values documented in Part I.
Everything built on that foundation may be refined.

[[P-8]]
==== § P.8 — Manual Structure

This manual is built in four Parts, eleven Articles, four Appendices, and back matter.
The order is deliberate: identity first, then what we do, then how we sustain it, then where we are going.
Each Part builds on the one before it.

*Part I — Identity* (Articles 1–2) answers the question: _who is {company-short}?_
Article 1 defines the legal entity, brand, and organizational foundation.
Article 2 details the publishing house model — the way we engage with creators and the reason the <<def-company,Company>> is structured the way it is.

*Part II — Operations* (Articles 3–5) answers the question: _what does {company-short} do?_
Article 3 documents the Bereshit technology stack, from the foundational layers all the way up through applications.
Article 4 covers the services we provide to creatives, businesses, and the body of Christ.
Article 5 identifies who we serve and where we draw the boundaries of that service.

*Part III — Business* (Articles 6–7) answers the question: _how does {company-short} sustain itself?_
Article 6 covers revenue, economic philosophy, and financial governance.
Article 7 defines the core values — the non-negotiables that govern every decision and relationship within the <<def-company,Company>>.

*Part IV — Direction* (Articles 8–11) answers the question: _where is {company-short} going?_
Article 8 contains the mission and vision.
Article 9 introduces the team and <<def-board,Board>> structure.
Article 10 maps the timeline from foundation through the Millennium.
Article 11 brings everything together — complete synthesis, with summaries written for <<def-board,the Board>>, <<def-team-members,Team Members>>, and <<def-partners,Partners>> specifically.

Four Appendices support the Articles: a Document Map, a Scripture Index, a Decision Frameworks collection, and an Anti-Patterns Catalog.
These are reference tools — they exist so that the Articles themselves stay focused on substance.

[[P-10]]
==== § P.10 — Manual Maintenance

A vision that drifts is worse than a vision that was never written.
This manual stays current because we built the discipline to keep it current.

Every change is tracked in version control (Git).
Each Article carries its own version number and revision date.
Nothing is changed without a record — because accountability requires a trail.

Four events trigger review:

. *Annual Review* — Full review for currency, accuracy, and alignment with the <<def-company,Company>>'s mission.
. *Phase Reviews* — When {company-short} transitions between development phases, affected Articles are updated to reflect new realities.
. *Section Updates* — When operational changes, <<def-leadership,Leadership>> decisions, or <<def-board,Board>> resolutions require revision.
. *Content Additions* — When the <<def-company,Company>> grows into areas this <<def-manual,Manual>> does not yet address.

Substantive changes require <<def-board,Board>> approval.
Editorial corrections and clarifications may be made by the document maintainer and noted in the revision history.
The amendment procedures in Part IV govern the process — it is transparent, and the record is permanent.
