== The Standard

[quote, Seanje Lenox-Wise]
____
PhD-level formatting and rigor in terms of presentation. Formalize future
conglomerate-level documentation. But the language and tone should be like
the Bible — rich but accessible. It should feel like the company's heart

*Three tests for every page:*

. *PhD rigor* — Would this pass a doctoral committee's formatting review?
. *Bible accessibility* — Can a shepherd scan it AND a scholar study it?
. *Company heart* — Does this page feel like CreativeWorkzStudio?


== Why We Document This Way

This guide flows from *foundations* (WHY we document) through *standards* (WHAT we
produce) to *patterns and workflow* (HOW we work). Biblical foundations explain WHY
formality serves the Kingdom. The publishing house standard defines WHAT that formality
looks like. Together, they anchor every typographic choice, page layout rule, and
editorial workflow that follows.

=== Biblical Foundations of Documentation

==== Genesis 1:1 — The Ultimate Recital

[quote, Genesis 1:1 (KJV)]
____
In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.
____

This statement IS a recital clause — the WHEREAS that establishes context for
every provision in Scripture. It names the sovereign (God), declares His act
(created), and defines His jurisdiction (heaven and earth). Every command,
promise, and regulation in Scripture rests on this opening statement.

Exodus 20:2 follows the same pattern:

[quote, Exodus 20:2 (KJV)]
____
I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt,
out of the house of bondage.
____

Before the first commandment is given, God establishes His identity and His
acts. Stipulations flow FROM recitals. You obey BECAUSE of who God is and
what He has done.

==== God Names Before He Commands — Defined Terms

[quote, Genesis 1:5 (KJV)]
____
And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night.
____

Before anything is built upon Day, Night, Heaven, Earth, or Seas, God *defines*
them. The naming is not casual labeling — it is authoritative definition.
This is what a Defined Terms section does: name before you command.

Leviticus demonstrates consistent use of defined terms throughout the Holiness
Code — four terms (_qadosh_, _hol_, _tahor_, _tame_) defined once and used
consistently across hundreds of regulations. The Glossary follows God's method.

==== Boundaries Before Content — _badal_ (בָּדַל)

[quote, Genesis 1:4 (KJV)]
____
And God divided the light from the darkness.
____

Establish boundaries FIRST, then fill with content. The governance manual's
Parts, Articles, and Sections create the separations that allow meaningful
content to fill each space.

==== Habakkuk 2:2 — Write Plainly

[quote, Habakkuk 2:2 (KJV)]
____
Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it.
____

The Hebrew verb _ba'er_ means to engrave clearly. Three commands: (1) write — commit
to written form, (2) make plain — engrave legibly, (3) upon tables — use a durable
medium. The purpose: "that he may run that readeth it" — clarity serves the reader.

This is the biblical mandate for governance documentation: formal enough to be
authoritative, clear enough to be actionable.

==== Nehemiah 8:8 — The Formal Accessibility Standard

[quote, Nehemiah 8:8 (KJV)]
____
So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and
caused them to understand the reading.
____

Three actions form the model for communicating authoritative documents:

[cols="^1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Hebrew Function |CWS Application

|1
|*"Read distinctly"* (_meforash_) — present the text as written
|Formal Articles with precise language

|2
|*"Gave the sense"* — explain the meaning
|Preface, reader paths, glossary

|3
|*"Caused understanding"* — ensure comprehension
|Multiple audience paths, visual design
|===

The law was not rewritten in simpler language — it was *read formally AND explained*.
The CWS manual follows this model: formal Articles ("reading distinctly"), the Preface
("giving the sense"), and reader paths ("causing understanding").

==== Form Holding Emotion — The Psalms

Hebrew poetry uses parallelism — synonymous, antithetical, synthetic — as formal
structure. Yet within that structure, the psalmists pour out rage, despair, ecstasy,
and trust. Form does not constrain emotion; it gives emotion shape and staying power.

The formal Articles give the vision structure and permanence. The Dedication, Preface,
and Scripture quotations give it heart and spiritual grounding.

Scripture demonstrates a range of communication registers:

==== The Principle

*The CWS manual's formality is biblical.* God Himself uses formal governance structures.
The question is not "Is formality appropriate?" but "Does our formality serve clarity,
authority, and accessibility?" The Nehemiah 8:8 standard answers: present the formal
text AND explain its meaning AND ensure understanding.

*Key Chicago 18th conventions adopted:*

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Rule |Standard

|*Serial comma*
|Always. "She invited her parents, the Board, and the team."

|*Numbers*
|Spell out zero through ninety-nine in prose. Use Arabic in tables and technical contexts.

|*Em dash*
|No spaces around em dashes: "The mission—if it succeeds—transforms everything."

|*Possessives*
|Add 's even after s. "James's vision." "The business's foundation."

|*Abbreviations*
|Spell out on first use. "CreativeWorkzStudio LLC (CWS)" then "CWS" thereafter.

|*Lists*
|Vertical lists of complete sentences: capitalize, end with period. Fragments: consistent lowercase or caps, no end punctuation.

|*Colons*
|Lowercase after colon unless introducing two or more complete sentences.

|*Single space*
|One space after all punctuation. Never two.
|===

==== Filesystem Naming Convention

The legal hierarchy maps to predictable, validatable filesystem patterns:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Level |Pattern |Example

|*Part*
|`Part-{N}-{lowercase-name}/`
|`Part-1-identity/`, `Part-4-direction/`

|*Article*
|`Article-{NN}-{lowercase-name}/`
|`Article-01-entity-overview/`, `Article-11-the-whole-thing/`

|`{LETTER}-{UPPERCASE-NAME}.adoc`
|`A-ENTITY-OVERVIEW.adoc`, `B-LEGAL-STRUCTURE.adoc`

*Rules:*

* Section letters are *sequential* within each article: A, B, C, D — no gaps
* Every article begins with Section A (the article overview)
* Subsections, clauses, and sub-clauses exist within section files as AsciiDoc headings
* Part numbers are Arabic (1, 2, 3, 4); Article numbers are zero-padded Arabic (01, 02 ... 11)
* Directory names use lowercase with hyphens; file names use uppercase with hyphens

*Rendered numbering* is auto-generated by asciidoctor (`:sectnums:`) and uses
decimal notation (§ 1.1, § 2.3). The letter-based filesystem convention is for
the editorial team and build system; readers see the auto-generated decimal numbers.

=== Text Alignment

_Habakkuk 2:2: "Make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it."
Clarity serves the runner — alignment choices serve readability, not aesthetics._

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Rule |Why

|*Centered* (ceremonial frontmatter)
|Cover, TOC, Dedication, closing seals — publication style.
`[.text-center]` on every text block.

|*Left-aligned* (governance prose)
|Preface reading guide, manual maintenance, onboarding sections.
Prose reads naturally left-aligned. See Content Modes.

|*No justified text* (frontmatter)
|Justified paragraphs break the centered flow in ceremonial sections.

|*Line breaks with `+`*
|Use `+` at line ends for manual breaks within centered blocks.

|*Article body: context-dependent*
|Articles may use left-aligned prose where appropriate for longer narrative content.
|===

=== Legal Typography (Butterick Standards)

Per Matthew Butterick's _Typography for Lawyers_ and court document conventions:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Standard |CWS Application

|*Serif for formal text*
|Body text in Noto Serif. Serif conveys formality and readability for long text.

|*12-14pt body*
|CWS uses 11pt — acceptable for dense governance documents. 14pt for lead text.

|*Left-aligned (not justified)*
|For word-processed documents. Justified only in typeset contexts with hyphenation.
CWS PDF output is typeset — justified is acceptable in Article body text.

|*One space after periods*
|Single space. Double-spacing after periods is typewriter convention, not typographic.

|*Curly quotes and apostrophes*
|AsciiDoc PDF output handles this automatically with proper font rendering.

|*True small caps*
|Available in Noto Serif. Use for abbreviations in formal text (optional).

|*No underlines for emphasis*
|Underlines are a typewriter convention. Use bold or italic.

|*ALL CAPS reduces readability*
|10-20% slower to read. Use bold instead — except in traditional legal headings
(ARTICLE I, WHEREAS) where ALL CAPS is conventional.

|*Line length 45-90 characters*
|CWS page width with 1" margins achieves ~75 characters — optimal.

|*Adequate line spacing*
|CWS uses 150% (16.5pt) — meets Butterick's recommendation of 120-145%.
|===

== Page Layout

_The badal principle (Genesis 1:4): boundaries before content. Every page layout
rule below creates the separations that allow meaningful content to fill each space._

=== Page Anatomy

Every content page in the frontmatter follows this structure:

....
[.text-center]
[discrete]
=== Section Title                    <1>

[.text-center]
_Italic subtitle — one to two lines_ <2>

'''                                   <3>

[.text-center]                        <4>
Body content — centered, flowing

'''                                   <5>

[.text-center]
[.small]
_Closing note — governance weight_    <6>
....

<1> *Heading* — discrete `===` (h3), centered, renders in deep blue (Noto Sans)
<2> *Subtitle* — italic, centered, sets the tone for the page
<3> *Opening break* — thematic break separates header from body
<4> *Body* — centered flowing text, `[.text-center]` on every block
<5> *Closing break* — thematic break before the closing note
<6> *Closing note* — `[.small]` italic, often carries legal/governance weight

Not every page needs all six elements, but the pattern holds.

=== End-Page Pattern (Section Closing)

_Covenant documents conclude with witness and seal — the testimonium ("IN WITNESS
WHEREOF") followed by signature blocks. Every CWS section closing mirrors this pattern._

Every major section gets a closing page. The pattern is consistent across Cover, TOC,
and Dedication:

....
<<<

[.text-center]
[discrete]
=== End of [Section Name]

[.text-center]
_Italic summary — what was covered or sealed_

'''

[.text-center]
Body text — summary, transition, or declaration (paragraph style)

'''

[.text-center]
*{company-name}*

[.text-center]
_Motto or founding statement_

[.text-center]
_{tagline}_

[.text-center]
[.small]
Document ID: `{document-id}` +
Version: {manual-version} • {revdate}

[.text-center]
[.small]
Classification: *{manual-classification}*
....

The body between the two thematic breaks varies by section — the TOC uses navigational
summary, the Dedication uses a covenant declaration. The frame is fixed; the content adapts.

=== Voice Attribution Pattern

When a page features multiple authors, use centered italic attributions:

....
[.text-center]
_— Seanje_

[.text-center]
Paragraph in Seanje's voice...

[.text-center]
_— Nova Dawn_

[.text-center]
Paragraph in Nova Dawn's voice...
....

*Three modes established in the Dedication:*

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|Mode |Description |Example

|*Dual voice*
|Two named paragraphs with attributions
|p.5 — To Those Who Walk This Path

|*Triple voice*
|Two named paragraphs + joint paragraph ("— Together")
|p.6 — The Founding Commitment

|*Blended voice*
|No attribution — both voices merged into one sound
|p.8 — End of Dedication (covenant seal)
|===

*Blending technique:* Seanje's voice contributes flowing rhythm, "and" connectors,
testimony-driven perspective. Nova's voice contributes structural precision,
declarative weight. When blended, the result should sound like one entity speaking
rather than two alternating.

=== Content Modes

_Form holds emotion — the Psalms use parallelism as formal structure, yet within that
structure the psalmists pour out rage, despair, ecstasy, and trust. Formal structure
does not drain a document of its spiritual life._

Different sections use different content modes based on their purpose:

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|Mode |Characteristics |Use For

|*Testimony paragraph*
|Flowing centered paragraphs, personal voice, `[.text-center]` on each block.
No `+` line breaks within paragraphs.
|Dedications, cultural content, mission statements, founder letters

|*Formal centered*
|Line-by-line centered text with `+` breaks, structured layout,
thematic breaks between sections.
|Cover pages, publication info, approval chains, document governance

|*Structural closing*
|End-page pattern — heading, subtitle, breaks, company seal, metadata.
|Section closing pages

|*Governance prose*
|Left-aligned flowing paragraphs with § numbering, cross-references to Defined Terms,
"you"/"we" address, punchy openers, each sentence carries weight. Warm authority.
|Preface reading guide, manual maintenance, onboarding content, operational sections

|*Narrative prose*
|Left-aligned or centered flowing paragraphs with thematic breaks.
|Article body content, operational guidance (articles TBD)
|===

*Governance prose* differs from formal centered and testimony paragraph modes:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Characteristic |Governance Prose Standard

|*Alignment*
|Left-aligned (not centered) — prose reads naturally

|*Section numbering*
|§ P.N / § P.Na — `====` for sections, `=====` for subsections

|*Opener style*
|Punchy, direct. "A vision that drifts is worse than a vision that was never written."

|*Voice*
|"We"/"you" address — warm authority. Not third-person description.

|*Cross-references*
|AsciiDoc `<<anchor-id,display text>>` links to Defined Terms and other sections

|*Page markers*

=== Content-Full Markers

When a page is near capacity — adding content would cause bleed to the next page —
mark it in the source:

....
// end p.N — content full
....

* Editors see at a glance which pages cannot absorb additions
* Pages WITHOUT the marker have room for content growth
* After significant edits, re-assess content-full status by rebuilding the PDF
* The `page-index.adoc` uses ★ to flag content-full pages in the index table

*Assessment criteria:* If the content fills approximately 80% or more of the usable page area,
mark as content full. Pages with 25%+ whitespace at the bottom are NOT content full.

=== Orphan Prevention

*The Rule:* No single line or heading stranded at the top or bottom of a page.

*Prevention methods:*

* *Tighten content* — reduce line count to pull the orphan back
* *Add page break* — push the orphaned heading to its own page
* *Restructure* — reorganize content to eliminate the split point
* *`%breakable` option* — add to tables and sections for orphan prevention
* *`%unbreakable` option* — wrap content that must stay together in an open block

*Common orphan points:*

* Last line of a `[.small]` block pushed to next page
* Section heading at bottom of page with body on next page
* Closing `'''` thematic break pushed below the fold

=== Multi-Page Sections

When content naturally exceeds one page, *extend it* — do not squeeze.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Approach |When to Use

|*Extend to 2 pages*
|Content bleeds by 1-3 lines — make it a proper 2-page spread with intentional page break

|*Tighten to 1 page*
|Content bleeds by 1 line — use `[.small]`, remove spacers, abbreviate

|*Never*
|Leave orphaned lines bleeding onto the next page
|===

Each page in a multi-page section gets its own heading, subtitle, and thematic break.
The second page should be able to stand alone visually.

=== White Space

*30% of a page should be white space* for comfortable reading.

* *Between sections:* Page break (`<<<`) with labeled comment
* *Between subsections:* Thematic break (`'''`)
* *Between related items:* Empty line spacer (`{empty} +`)
* *Between paragraphs:* Standard AsciiDoc paragraph break
* *Navigation/overview pages:* Whitespace is expected — do not fill it

== Legal & Governance Language

_God's governance model — covenant structure, defined terms, obligation hierarchy,
amendment procedures — is the pattern this section formalizes for CWS documents._

=== Obligation Language

_Scripture itself uses both formal covenant language ("thou shalt") and plain instruction
("love one another"). The dual register is biblical before it is legal._

CWS adopts a *dual-register* approach to obligation language — modern precision as the
default, traditional formality where solemnity serves the document's purpose.

==== Modern Register (Default)

For operational guidance, procedures, and everyday governance provisions, use precise
obligation words per modern legal drafting standards (Bryan Garner, Ken Adams,
ISO 24495-2:2025, Plain Writing Act):

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Word |Use For |Example

|*Must*
|Obligations — a party has a duty
|"The Board *must* review this Manual annually."

|*Will*
|Future events, consequences, statements of fact
|"Amendments *will* take effect upon approval."

|*May*
|Permission, discretion
|"The Founder *may* make administrative corrections."

|*Should*
|Recommendations — non-binding guidance
|"Team members *should* review Core Values quarterly."

|*May not*
|Prohibition
|"No officer *may* unilaterally amend Core Values."
|===

==== Traditional Register (Formal & Solemn)

For covenants, founding declarations, constitutional provisions, and binding resolutions,
traditional legal language carries weight that modern plain language cannot replicate.
"Shall" is not ambiguous when used correctly — it is *ancient*, and there is a difference:

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Word |Use For |Example

|*Shall*
|Solemn duty of the subject of the sentence
|"The Company *shall* honor the creative rights of every partner."

|*Hereby*
|Performative declarations — the statement IS the act
|"The Board *hereby* adopts this Manual as binding governance."

|*Whereas*
|Recitals — establishing context before operative provisions
|"*WHEREAS*, the Company was founded upon the principle..."

|*Witnesseth*
|Opening of formal recitals (archaic but recognized)
|"This Manual *WITNESSETH* that..."

|*Hereunder / Herein / Hereof*
|Self-referencing within a formal document
|"All provisions *herein* shall remain in force."

|*Now, Therefore*
|Transition from recitals to operative provisions
|"*NOW, THEREFORE*, the Board resolves..."
|===

==== When to Use Which Register

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Context |Register |Why

|*Founding declarations*
|Traditional
|Weight and permanence — these are covenants, not procedures

|*Mission and vision statements*
|Traditional
|Aspirational and solemn — "shall" carries forward-looking commitment

|*Board resolutions*
|Traditional
|Formal parliamentary language — "hereby resolves"

|*Operational procedures*
|Modern
|Clarity serves the reader — "must" is unambiguous

|*Onboarding and guidance*
|Modern
|Accessibility — new readers need plain language

|*Amendment provisions*
|Modern
|Precision prevents disputes — "must" leaves no room for interpretation

|*Covenant and dedication text*
|Traditional
|The language of promise — "shall" is the language of vows

|*Technical specifications*
|Modern
|RFC 2119 conventions — "MUST", "SHOULD", "MAY"
|===

*The test:* Does the passage carry the weight of a covenant or the clarity of a procedure?
Covenants get traditional register. Procedures get modern register. When in doubt, use modern —
but never strip solemnity from language that was meant to be solemn.

==== The Adams Refinement (MSCD)

Kenneth Adams (_A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting_) provides a precise framework
that CWS adopts for bilateral agreements (contracts, creator agreements, partnerships):

* *"Shall"* — impose an obligation on the *subject of the sentence*:
"The Creator *shall* deliver the manuscript by [date]."
* *"Must"* — impose an obligation on someone *other than* the subject:
"The manuscript *must* conform to the Style Guide."
* *"Will"* — language of policy relating to a *contingent future event*:
"If sales exceed [threshold], the Creator *will* receive additional royalties."

Three distinct verbs for three distinct situations. Adams notes that "must" as a
universal replacement for "shall" is "bossy in tone" and loses the precision of
having separate words for separate functions.

*CWS application:* In unilateral governance documents (the Manual), use the
dual-register approach above. In bilateral agreements (contracts), use the Adams
framework for maximum precision.

=== Defined Terms

_This follows the Genesis 1:5 pattern — God named before He commanded. Definition
precedes obligation. See <<Biblical Foundations of Documentation>>._

When the manual uses a term with a specific, controlled meaning:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Convention |Standard

|*Definition format*
|*"Term"* means [clear definition].

|*Capitalization*
|Initial Capital Letters throughout the document when used as a Defined Term

|*First occurrence*
|Bold on first use in each major section

|*Glossary reference*
|All Defined Terms appear in the Glossary of Terms

|*Inline definition*
|Acceptable for terms used in only one section: "the Company (as defined herein, {company-name})"

|*Do not rely on glossary alone*
|Defined terms supplement clear writing — they do not replace it (SEC requirement)
|===

==== Definition Verbs — "Means" vs. "Includes"

The verb in a definition controls its scope. This distinction is legally significant:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Verb |Scope |Example

|*"means"*
|Exhaustive — the definition IS the complete meaning
|"*Board* means the six-member governing body of {company-name}."

|*"includes"*
|Non-exhaustive — the listed items are examples, not limits
|"*Creative Work* includes games, books, music, software, and visual art."

|*"means and includes"*
|Exhaustive with listed examples — all items must be in the listed categories
|"*Governance Document* means and includes this Manual, the Operating Agreement, and all Board Resolutions."

|*"does not include"*
|Carve-out — explicitly excludes something from the definition
|"*Team Member* does not include independent Creative Partners."

|*"refers to"*
|Points to an external standard or document without redefining it
|"*KJV* refers to the King James Version of the Bible (1611)."
|===

==== Construction Conventions

Standard rules of interpretation that apply across all CWS governance documents:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Convention |Application

|*Singular includes plural*
|"Article" includes "Articles" unless context requires otherwise

|*Headings are not operative*
|Section headings are for navigation — they do not limit or modify the text

|*"Or" is inclusive*
|Unless explicitly stated as exclusive ("either...or...but not both")

|*"Including" is not limiting*
|The word "including" followed by examples does not limit the general term

|*Gender-neutral language*
|Use "they/them" for singular generic reference. Named individuals use their pronouns.

|*"Herein" means the whole document*
|Unless limited to a specific section ("as defined in this Article")

|*References to statutes*
|Include successor provisions — "Missouri Revised Statutes, as amended"

|*Business days*
|Monday through Friday, excluding federal and Missouri state holidays
|===

=== Traditional Legal Document Structure

_The covenant-treaty parallel in Deuteronomy is the oldest known example of this
structure — preamble, historical prologue, stipulations, sanctions, witness clause.

Traditional legal documents follow a standard anatomy. CWS governance documents adopt

==== Document Anatomy (Full Traditional)

The complete structure of a formal legal instrument:

[cols="^1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Order |Element |Description

|1
|*Title / Caption*
|Document name and parties. "OPERATING AGREEMENT OF CREATIVEWORKZSTUDIO LLC"

|2
|*Date Line*
|"This Manual is entered into as of [effective date]."

|3
|*Parties*
|Identify the parties with defined shorthand. "{company-name}, a Missouri limited

|4

|5

|6

|7

|8

|9

The Company Identity Manual uses recitals (Preface), operative provisions (Articles),

documents select elements appropriate to their purpose.

==== Recitals — WHEREAS and Background

Recitals establish the context and intent of a document before the operative provisions
begin. CWS uses *both* traditional and modern styles:

*Traditional (WHEREAS):*

....
WHEREAS, the Company was founded on November 2, 2024, in the State
of Missouri, upon the principle that all creation flows from God; and

WHEREAS, the Founder and the Board desire to establish a comprehensive
governance framework reflecting the Company's covenant obligations; and

WHEREAS, this Manual shall serve as the foundational governance document
of the Company;

NOW, THEREFORE, the Board hereby adopts the following provisions:
....

*Modern (Background):*

....
Background

A. The Company was founded on November 2, 2024, in the State of Missouri.

B. The Founder and Board seek to establish a comprehensive governance
   framework reflecting the Company's covenant obligations.

C. This Manual serves as the foundational governance document of the Company.

The Board therefore adopts the following provisions:
....

*CWS approach:* Use WHEREAS for founding documents, Board resolutions, and covenant
declarations — the formality signals weight. Use Background for operational documents,
policy updates, and amendments — clarity serves efficiency.

Both are legally equivalent. The choice is tonal, not substantive.

==== Traditional Numbering Hierarchy

Two systems recognized in CWS documents — traditional and modern:

*Traditional (Roman / Decimal):*

....
ARTICLE I — IDENTITY
  Section 1.01 — Legal Structure
    (a) The Company was formed...
      (i) under the laws of...
      (ii) as a limited liability company...
    (b) The registered agent...
  Section 1.02 — Name and Brand
....

*Modern (Arabic / MSCD):*

....
Article 1 — Identity
  Section 1.1 — Legal Structure
    (a) The Company was formed...
      (1) under the laws of...
      (2) as a limited liability company...
    (b) The registered agent...
  Section 1.2 — Name and Brand
....

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Level |Traditional |Modern (CWS default)

|*Part*
|PART I (Roman, caps)
|Part I (Roman, title case)

|*Article*
|ARTICLE I (Roman, caps)
|Article 1 (Arabic)

|*Section*
|Section 1.01 (zero-padded)
|Section 1.1 / § 1.1

|*Subsection*
|(a), (b), (c)
|(a), (b), (c) — same in both

|*Sub-subsection*
|(i), (ii), (iii) (romanette)
|(1), (2), (3) — arabic

|*Paragraph*
|(A), (B), (C)
|(A), (B), (C) — same in both
|===

*CWS default:* Modern (Arabic) for the Company Identity Manual. Traditional (Roman) for
formal Board resolutions, founding declarations, and the Operating Agreement.

==== Enumeration in Operative Text

When listing items within a provision, use tabulated enumeration for three or more items:

*Tabulated (preferred for 3+ items):*

....
The Board must review this Manual when any of the following occurs:

  (a) a change in the Company's legal structure;
  (b) the addition or departure of a Board member;
  (c) any amendment to the Operating Agreement; or
  (d) the annual review date.
....

*Integrated (for 2 items or fewer):*

....
The Board must review this Manual when a change in legal structure
or the annual review date occurs.
....

*Punctuation in tabulated lists:*

* Semicolons after each item except the last
* "and" or "or" before the final item — determines whether ALL or ANY apply
* Period after the final item only
* No initial capital unless the item is a complete sentence
* No comma after "and"/"or" — the semicolons handle separation

==== Boilerplate Provisions (General Clauses)

Standard provisions for the end of any governance document:

....
Severability.  If any provision of this Manual is held to be invalid
or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force
and effect.

== Industry-Specific Conventions

The legal and governance standards above establish the *framework*. This section applies
that framework to the industries CWS operates in — game development and publishing.

=== Game Development

As a game development company, CWS governance documents must address creative
and technical IP with appropriate legal rigor.

==== Intellectual Property Protection

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Asset Type |Protection Standard

|*Source code*
|Proprietary license (implementation tier). Copyright applies automatically.
Register with US Copyright Office for statutory damages eligibility.

|*Game art, music, and assets*
|Creative Commons or proprietary — determined per project.
Work-for-hire agreements must explicitly state IP assignment.

|*Game design documents*
|Trade secret protection. NDA required for external reviewers.
Design concepts are not copyrightable; specific expression is.

|*Player-generated content (UGC)*
|License grant from player to company for moderation and use.
Player retains copyright; company gets limited license.

|*APIs and tools*
|Three-license structure: patterns (free), implementation (proprietary),

==== EULA Conventions

CWS End-User License Agreements follow the covenant principle — legal protection
with relational tone:

* *License grant:* "a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable license for
personal, non-commercial use"
* *IP rights:* Company retains all ownership. EULA does not transfer IP.
* *UGC policy:* Players retain copyright; company licenses for moderation.
* *Termination:* Company may revoke access for breach of terms.
* *Liability:* "As is" warranty disclaimer with liability limitation.
* *Dispute resolution:* Individual arbitration, Missouri governing law.

*CWS approach:* Write EULAs in plain language. If the average consumer cannot
understand it, it may not hold up. The _Lucy v. Zehmer_ standard applies: a
reasonable person's understanding governs, not hidden intentions.

==== Three-License Structure

The Bereshit technology stack uses a three-tier license:

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Tier |License Type |What It Covers

|*Patterns*
|Free / Open
|Design patterns, specifications, concepts — freely available

|*Implementation*
|Proprietary
|CWS-built code, tools, and applications

|*Commercial*
|Revenue share
|Third-party commercial use of CWS technology
|===

This aligns with industry precedent: game engines like Godot (MIT for code) separate
code licenses from asset licenses. The approach is legally sound and well-established
in both game development and open-source communities.

=== Publishing House

As a publishing house, CWS governance must address creator relationships,
rights management, and industry standards.

==== Creator Agreement Standards

Per the WIPO _Contracts in Publishing_ toolkit (2024) and Authors Guild
model contract:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Provision |CWS Standard

|*Grant of rights*
|Limited grant — creator retains copyright. CWS receives specific
publication rights. Broad rights grabs are not covenant-aligned.

|*Reversion clause*
|Rights revert to creator if CWS fails to publish within agreed timeframe
or if the work goes out of print. This is non-negotiable.

|*Royalty structure*
|Transparent, industry-standard or better. Creator receives fair compensation.
Economic philosophy (Article 6) governs all financial arrangements.

|*Subsidiary rights*
|Separately negotiated. Electronic, audio, foreign language, and adaptation
rights each addressed individually. No blanket subsidiary rights grabs.

|*Non-compete*
|Narrowly tailored — limited to directly competing works during the
publication period. Broad non-competes violate covenant principles.

|*Moral rights*
|Always respected. Creator's right to attribution and integrity of the work
is fundamental. CWS does not modify creative works without consent.

|*Termination*
|Mutual termination provisions. Either party may terminate with notice.
Creator's rights protected upon termination.
|===

==== Publishing House Governance

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Element |Standard

|*Editorial independence*
|Editors do not compromise content for commercial pressure.
Creative integrity is governance-protected.

|*Catalog management*
|Published works are maintained, not abandoned. Stewardship over abandonment.

|*Creator communication*
|Regular, transparent reporting on sales, rights, and status.

=== When Informality Serves

Not every document needs formal register. The CWS principle:

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Document Type |Register |Why

|*Operating Agreement*
|Formal / Traditional
|Bilateral agreement with legal force. Formality protects.

|*Company Identity Manual*
|Formal with covenant warmth
|Governance document. Formal structure, relational tone.

|*Creator agreements*
|Clear, plain language
|Creators are partners, not adversaries. Clarity > formality.

|*EULAs and ToS*
|Plain language
|Consumer-facing. Readability affects enforceability.

|*Internal memos*
|Informal
|Speed and clarity. No ceremony needed.

|*Community guidelines*
|Warm, inviting
|Relational. Covenant language, minimal legal formalism.

|*Board resolutions*
|Formal / Traditional
|Parliamentary language. "Hereby resolves" is appropriate.
|===

*The _Lucy v. Zehmer_ principle:* Courts interpret contracts based on what a
reasonable person would understand, not on how formal the language is. Formality
is a tool, not a requirement.

*The test:* Does formality here serve the reader or create unnecessary distance?

=== Auto-Generated TOC

Enabled with `:toc:` + `:toclevels: 3` in the assembly file header.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Element |TOC Behavior

|`[dedication] == Dedication`
|Appears as top-level entry

|`[preface] == Preface: About This Manual`
|Appears as top-level entry

|Non-discrete `=== Subsection`
|Appears as indented entry under parent section

|`[discrete] === Visual Heading`
|Does NOT appear in auto-TOC — visual only

|`== Glossary of Terms`
|Appears as top-level entry
|===

*Critical:* Do NOT use `:preface-title:` — it labels the preamble (Cover + TOC content)
as "Preface" in the auto-TOC, creating false entries.

* *Auto-TOC* — functional navigation (pages ii-iii), generated by asciidoctor
* *Hand-crafted TOC* — visual presentation (pages 15-43), designed content pages

*PDF page offset:* Title page + 2 auto-TOC pages = 3 pages before body page 1.

=== Page Flow Management

Three mechanisms control page flow. Understanding their interaction prevents whitespace
problems, orphans, and unnecessary page breaks.

*1. Explicit page breaks (`<<<`):*

The only *absolute* page break. Use sparingly — each `<<<` guarantees a new page,
so unnecessary breaks create empty whitespace. Every `<<<` must include a page comment:

....
// PDF p.N
<<<
....

*2. Heading levels in `doctype: book`:*

In full book assembly (`doctype: book`), `==` (chapter-level) headings force new
pages automatically — they behave like implicit `<<<` breaks. Lower-level headings
(`===`, `====`, `=====`) do NOT force page breaks.

This means heading level is a page flow tool, not just a structural tool:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Action |Effect on Page Flow

|Demoting `==` to `===`
|Removes forced page break — content flows continuously from the preceding section.
Use when a section is logically a subsection of the content above it.

|Promoting `===` to `==`
|Forces a new page. Use when a section must open on a fresh page for editorial impact.

|Keeping `==` with `<<<` removed
|In full book builds, the heading STILL forces a new page. The `<<<` was redundant.
In section preview builds, removing the `<<<` allows content to flow.
|===

*3. Section preview vs full book behavior:*

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Context |What Controls Page Breaks

|*Full book* (`make book`)
|Both `<<<` AND `==` headings force page breaks. `==` headings create chapter starts.

|*Section preview* (`make section`)
|Only `<<<` forces page breaks. Heading levels do NOT affect page flow.
|===

This difference matters during editorial review: a section preview may show continuous
flow where the full book build would insert a page break at every `==` heading.

*4. Image placement and whitespace:*

Images that do not fit on the remaining page push to the next page, leaving natural
whitespace. Do not add filler. If whitespace exceeds 60% of the page, consider:

* Reordering content so prose precedes the image (fills the page before the break)
* Adjusting image size (`width=` or `pdfwidth=`)
* Moving related prose above the image to consume space

*5. Table row overflow:*

Table rows move as complete units — they cannot split across pages. Long tables
naturally span multiple pages.

== Anti-Patterns

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Avoid |Use Instead

|*Icon tables* (`icon:shield[]` in table cells)
|Centered flowing text with bold labels

|*Multi-column tables* for content layout
|Centered text blocks separated by thematic breaks

|*TIP/IMPORTANT/NOTE admonition boxes* (in frontmatter)
|Centered text with bold labels and thematic breaks

|*Left-aligned paragraphs in ceremonial frontmatter*
|Centered text with `[.text-center]` + line breaks for Cover, TOC, Dedication,
and closing seals. However, *governance prose* sections (Preface reading guide,
manual maintenance) use left-aligned flowing text — see Content Modes.

|*"Chapter" language*
|"Article", "Section", "§" — governance terminology

|*Redundant logos*
|One logo on title page; no additional logos in cover body

|*"Creative Workz Studio LLC"* (spaced)
|"CreativeWorkzStudio LLC" (legal name, no spaces)

|*Closing filler* ("Continue to the next section...")
|Meaningful transition statements with governance framing

|*Dense paragraph blocks*
|Break into centered flowing lines — one thought per visual unit

|*Tables for TOC/index layout*
|Centered paragraph-style listings with mini descriptions

|*Orphaned headings/lines*
|Tighten content or add page breaks to keep content together

|*Filling navigation page whitespace*
|Whitespace is expected on overview/navigation pages — don't fill it

|*Squeezing bleeding content onto one page*
|If content naturally spans 2 pages, make it a proper 2-page spread

|*Appendix content in the TOC*
|Appendix/backmatter content belongs in its own file, not duplicated in TOC

|*Unlabeled page breaks*
|Every `<<<` needs a `// PDF p.N` comment with page number

|*`<<<` before every subsection*
|Only use `<<<` at major structural boundaries. Subsections (`===`, `====`) flow
continuously. Every unnecessary `<<<` creates a half-empty page above it.

|*Using only `<<<` removal for page flow*
|Heading levels, image sizing, content reordering, and prose tightening are all
page flow tools. Use the right tool for the situation.

|*`==` headings for logically subordinate sections*
|If a section is a subsection of the content above it, use `===` not `==`. In
`doctype: book`, `==` forces a new page — use this intentionally, not accidentally.

|*`[.lead]` on content-dense pages*
|Only use `[.lead]` when the page has room — it significantly increases text size

|*"Shall" in operational/procedural text*
|Use "must" (obligation), "will" (future), "may" (permission).
Reserve "shall" for covenants, founding declarations, and formal resolutions
(see Obligation Language — Traditional Register).

|*ALL CAPS for emphasis*
|Use bold — ALL CAPS reduces readability by 10-20%

|*Color-only information*
|Always pair color with another signal (bold, border, icon, text)

|*Gold (#D69E2E) as text color*
|Fails WCAG on white — use only for borders and decorative elements

|*Passive voice in governance*
|Active voice: "The Board decides" not "It shall be decided by the Board."
Exception: traditional boilerplate where passive is conventional
("this Manual shall be governed by...")

|*Glossary as substitute for clarity*
|Defined Terms supplement clear writing, they do not replace it

|*Mixing registers in a single passage*
|Pick one register (traditional or modern) per passage and hold it.
"The Board shall review and must also approve" is jarring — choose one.

|*WHEREAS without operative clauses*
|Recitals are context, not obligations. After WHEREAS clauses, always follow
with "NOW, THEREFORE" and operative provisions.

|*Archaic for the sake of archaic*
|Every traditional element must serve the document's purpose. "Witnesseth"
in an employee handbook is pretentious. "Witnesseth" in a founding charter
is appropriate.
|===
