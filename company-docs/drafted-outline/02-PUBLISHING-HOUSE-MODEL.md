## The Core Model

Creative Workz Studio operates as a **publishing house**—not a traditional tech company, not a studio that only makes its own products, but a publisher that serves creatives while also creating original works.

This model has three pillars:

1. **Publishing for External Creatives** — helping others bring their work to market
2. **Publishing Internal IPs** — creating and distributing our own intellectual properties
3. **Collaboration and Partnership** — working together across the network

---

## Pillar One: External Creatives

### The Core Principle: Creators Keep Their IP

**CWS does not take intellectual property rights from creators.**

This is fundamental. When a creative partners with CWS to publish their work:
- The creator **retains full ownership** of their intellectual property
- CWS provides services in exchange for a **revenue share**
- The creator maintains **autonomy** over their creation

This inverts the typical publisher model where the publisher owns or controls the IP. We believe creators should own what they create.

### What CWS Provides

When working with external creatives, CWS offers:

| Service | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Marketing** | Strategy, campaigns, audience development |
| **Publication** | Production, formatting, platform distribution |
| **Consultation** | Industry connections, strategic guidance |
| **Distribution** | Access to platforms, networks, and markets |
| **Infrastructure** | Tools built on Kingdom Technology stack |

### Revenue Split Model

The level of CWS investment determines the revenue split:

| CWS Investment Level | CWS Revenue Share | Creator Revenue Share |
|---------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Full Support | Higher % | Lower % |
| Moderate Support | Medium % | Medium % |
| Light Support | Lower % | Higher % |
| Infrastructure Only | Minimal % | Majority % |

**The creator chooses** the level of support they need. More support means higher CWS share; less support means the creator keeps more.

This preserves autonomy: creators aren't forced into one-size-fits-all contracts.

### What CWS Does NOT Do

- Take ownership of creator IP
- Lock creators into exclusive arrangements (unless mutually agreed)
- Require creators to use CWS infrastructure
- Penalize creators for working with others

## Pillar Three: Collaboration

### With External Creatives

CWS internal IPs can intersect with external creator IPs through:

- **Joint ventures** — Co-created properties
- **Licensing** — Use of CWS technology in creator projects
- **Cross-promotion** — Mutual marketing support
- **Shared infrastructure** — Common tools and platforms

### With Faith-Based Businesses

CWS partners with other Kingdom-aligned businesses:

- Providing technology infrastructure
- Mutual referrals and trade relationships
- Shared distribution networks
- Barter and trade arrangements

### With the Broader Network

CWS serves as connective tissue, not the center:

- Facilitating connections between creatives
- Enabling faith-based business collaboration
- Building infrastructure others can use
- Supporting the body of Christ to build

## Why This Model?

### Economic Resilience

The publishing house model creates multiple revenue streams:
- Revenue shares from many creators reduce single-point-of-failure risk
- Internal IPs provide owned assets
- Services generate consistent income
- Trade networks reduce dependency on monetary transactions

### Kingdom Alignment

- Creators keeping IP honors their work and calling
- Autonomy respects individual direction from the Lord
- Collaboration builds the body rather than competing
- Infrastructure serves rather than extracts

### Mark-Free Preparation

When economic systems require marks to participate:
- Trade networks provide alternatives
- Multiple creatives and businesses support each other
- Decentralized model resists central control
- Infrastructure doesn't depend on beast-system platforms
