= structural-scaffolding

*Definition:* Minimal structure (block headers, section markers) maintained during freeform development that enables later refinement. The skeleton that survives iteration.

[quote, Ezra 5:16 KJV]
____
Then came the same Sheshbazzar, and laid the foundation of the house of God which is in Jerusalem: and since that time even until now hath it been in building, and yet it is not finished.
____

== The Insight

Keep block headers even when content is messy. The headers are *anchors* — when refinement time comes, the structure is already there waiting. The skeleton survives the freeform phase.

== What It Is

* Block headers (SETUP, BODY, CLOSING) maintained always
* Section markers even with incomplete content
* Comments that name what belongs there
* Structure as roadmap for future work
* Bones that flesh gets added to later

== What It Is NOT

* Fully polished structure from the start
* No structure until "ready"
* Perfect content required for structure
* Structure that fights organic growth

== Why It Works

Refinement needs something to refine *against*. If you maintain the skeleton, you always know where things belong — even when the content is rough. Structure doesn't fight organic growth; it *channels* it.

== cws-struct Transform: Scaffolding Automated

The cws-struct transformer *is* structural scaffolding made automatic. A schema defines what the scaffold should look like. The DAR engine (Detect-Assess-Recover) reads the file, detects missing structure, and recovers by generating the scaffold — block headers, section markers, reserved omission comments, block overviews. What used to be maintained by discipline is now generated by tooling. The skeleton doesn't just survive iteration; it's produced from a single source of truth (the schema) and can be regenerated whenever the standard evolves.
