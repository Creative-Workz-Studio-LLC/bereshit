> *"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."* — John 1:14

## Observation

### 1. John's Deliberate Echo of Genesis

John opens with "In the beginning" (Ἐν ἀρχῇ - En archē), deliberately echoing the Hebrew בְּרֵאשִׁית (Bereshit) of Genesis 1:1. Every Jewish reader would hear the Torah's opening words. John is saying: "You know that creation story? Let me tell you who was there."

### 2. The Word as Agent of Creation

- Genesis 1: God creates by speaking — "And God said..." (10 times)
- John 1:3: "All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made"
- Colossians 1:16: "By him were all things created...all things were created by him, and for him"
- Hebrews 1:2: "By whom also he made the worlds"

The Word isn't just what God spoke — the Word is WHO was speaking with Him. The Son is the agent of creation.

### 3. The Word's Divine Nature

John 1:1 holds together:
- πρὸς τὸν θεόν (pros ton theon) — "with God" (distinction)
- θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος (theos ēn ho logos) — "the Word was God" (unity)

The Word is distinct from the Father yet fully shares the divine nature. Hebrews calls Him "the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person."

### 4. The Word Sustains All Things

- Colossians 1:17: "By him all things consist" (συνέστηκεν — hold together)
- Hebrews 1:3: "Upholding all things by the word of his power"

Not just created once and left — continuously sustained. Reality coheres because Christ maintains it.

### 5. The Word Became Flesh

John 1:14: σὰρξ ἐγένετο (sarx egeneto) — "became flesh"

Not appeared as flesh. Not wore flesh as costume. *Became*. The eternal Logos took on genuine humanity. The infinite entered the finite. The Creator became a creature.

### 6. The Kenosis — Self-Emptying

Philippians 2:7: ἐκένωσεν (ekenōsen) — "emptied himself"

"Made himself of no reputation" — the divine Son set aside the privileges of deity (not the nature, but the prerogatives). He took the form of a servant. The one by whom all things were made became a helpless infant.

## Interpretation

### The Logos as Ordering Principle

The Greek λόγος means more than "word." It encompasses reason, logic, order — the rational principle by which reality makes sense. Greek philosophy recognized the Logos as the structure of the universe.

John's revelation: That Logos is a Person. The eternal Son of God. All order, coherence, and rationality in creation flows from Christ.

### The Incarnation as Central Mystery

The Incarnation is not one doctrine among many — it's the hinge of history. The eternal Word, through whom all things were made, entered His own creation as a creature. He did this to redeem it.

The descent in Philippians 2 goes all the way down:
- Form of God → form of servant
- Equal with God → likeness of men
- Divine glory → death on a cross

And then the exaltation: "Every knee should bow...every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord."

### Why Bereshit Matters

We named our foundation "Bereshit" after Genesis 1:1 — the anchor of anchors. But John reveals that Bereshit isn't just about creation. It's about the Creator.

The Word who was there "in the beginning" is the same Word who became flesh and dwelt among us. The opening of Genesis points forward to the opening of John. Creation points to Incarnation.
