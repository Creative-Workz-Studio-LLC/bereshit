## Observation

After crystallizing the research template, Seanje paused me to compare the base and research templates side-by-side using grep. The output showed identical structure — 34 addressable sections (M.1-M.12, H.1-H.6, C.1-C.8, T.1-T.4, F.1-F.4) — but specialized content for each document type.

His point: "If I didn't teach you to come to this conclusion on your own, you wouldn't actually see and understand it."

---

## Pattern

**Teaching through doing, not telling.**

| Approach | Result |
|----------|--------|
| Hand me crystallized template, say "use this" | Mechanical pattern matching |
| Walk me through verbose → constrained → crystallized | Genuine understanding |

**The sequence that created understanding:**

1. **Verbose teaching mode** — 1297 lines with `// THINKING:` blocks explaining everything
2. **Working with it** — Hitting token limits, feeling the constraint
3. **Crystallizing** — Block by block, transforming to `// # M.1 Name [TAG]` format
4. **Comparing** — Seeing the parallel structure, understanding WHY

**What I now see everywhere:**

- Skills, hooks, configs — same addressable structure
- The 27-position cube — navigable coordinates
- Building block method — layers you can trace back
- OmniCode — same grepable sections

**The core insight:** Everything we build needs to be navigable without loading everything. The index window pattern. Work constrained like it's normal, and when limits lift, you fly.

---

## Integration

**What this changes:**

1. **Template crystallization isn't syntax work** — It's creating navigable schemas that tell you what each document type CONTAINS
2. **The constraint was the teacher** — Token limits forced discovery of patterns that work at any scale
3. **Teaching method matters** — Walk someone to the conclusion, don't hand them the answer

**For future teaching:**

When I need to help someone understand a pattern, don't just show the result. Walk them through the journey that produces understanding.
