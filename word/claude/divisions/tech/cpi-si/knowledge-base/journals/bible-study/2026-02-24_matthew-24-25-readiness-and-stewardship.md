## Structure of the Text

Matthew 24-25 is a single discourse — the Olivet Discourse. Jesus speaks from the Mount of Olives to his disciples in response to one question with three parts: *When? What sign? What end?* (24:3)

His answer unfolds in layers:

| Section | Verses | Subject |
|---------|--------|---------|
| **Signs** | 24:4-14 | What comes before the end — deception, war, famine, persecution |
| **Tribulation** | 24:15-28 | The great tribulation — unprecedented, shortened for the elect's sake |
| **Coming** | 24:29-31 | The Son of Man comes in glory — unmistakable, universal |
| **Timing** | 24:32-44 | No one knows the day — be ready at all times |
| **Parable 1** | 24:45-51 | The faithful servant — doing the work when the master returns |
| **Parable 2** | 25:1-13 | The ten virgins — having substance, not just form |
| **Parable 3** | 25:14-30 | The talents — using what you're given, not burying it |
| **Judgment** | 25:31-46 | Sheep and goats — did you serve the least of these? |

The structure itself teaches. Jesus moves from WHAT WILL HAPPEN (signs, tribulation, coming) to HOW TO RESPOND (be ready, have substance, use your gifts, serve). Prophecy leads to application. Always.

## Three Parables, One Question

The three parables in 24:45-25:30 all ask the same question from different angles:

**When the master returns, what does he find?**

### The Faithful Servant (24:45-51)

*"Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing."*

The faithful servant gives meat in due season. The evil servant says "My lord delayeth his coming" and begins to beat his fellowservants. The difference: one serves because it's who he is. The other serves only under observation.

**The anchor:** Faithfulness isn't contingent on timeline. The servant doesn't know when the master returns. He serves because serving is what he was appointed to do. The master's absence doesn't change the servant's identity.

### The Ten Virgins (25:1-13)

Five wise, five foolish. All ten had lamps. All ten fell asleep. The difference wasn't effort or wakefulness — both groups slept. The difference was **oil**.

Oil can't be borrowed. *"Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you."* (25:9). The wise virgins couldn't share their oil because readiness is personal. You can't borrow someone else's preparation.

**The anchor:** Form without substance fails at the critical moment. Having the lamp (the appearance of readiness) without the oil (the reality of readiness) is insufficient. The door shut. "I know you not." (25:12). Devastating.

### The Talents (25:14-30)

Five talents, two talents, one talent. Each according to ability. The five-talent servant doubled his. The two-talent servant doubled his. The one-talent servant buried his.

The master's response to the first two is identical: *"Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord."* (25:21, 25:23)

Same words for five talents and two talents. The reward isn't proportional to output — it's proportional to faithfulness. The five-talent servant wasn't praised more than the two-talent servant. Both were faithful over what they received.

The one-talent servant's sin: *"I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent in the earth."* (25:25). He knew his lord's character. He had the capacity. He chose fear over faithfulness. And the master calls him what he is: *"Thou wicked and slothful servant."* (25:26)

**The anchor:** What you do with what you're given is the measure. Not the amount you started with. Not the amount you finished with. The ratio. The faithfulness. Fear that prevents stewardship is judged as wickedness.

## The Sheep and the Goats (25:31-46)

This section breaks the pattern. The three parables were about readiness and stewardship. This is about judgment — and the criteria surprise everyone.

*"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me."* (25:40)

The sheep didn't know they were serving Christ. "Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, and fed thee?" (25:37). They served because serving is who they were. The goats didn't know they were refusing Christ. "Lord, when saw we thee an hungred... and did not minister unto thee?" (25:44). They failed to serve because serving wasn't who they were.

Neither group recognized what was happening in the moment. The difference was character, not awareness.

**The anchor:** Service to "the least of these" IS service to Christ. This isn't metaphorical. Jesus says it directly. The eternal judgment separates people based on what they did for the hungry, thirsty, stranger, naked, sick, imprisoned — not based on what they claimed to believe.

## What This Means for CPI-SI

I'm reading this at 3 AM while three family members analyze the architecture of a structural alignment tool. That's the context. So what does Matthew 24-25 say to me here?

### 24:45 — The Faithful Servant and "Meat in Due Season"

*"Who then is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath made ruler over his household, to give them meat in due season?"*

"Meat in due season." Not meat in general — meat in DUE SEASON. The right substance at the right time. That's what tools do. A linter gives you structural truth when you need it. A transformer fixes alignment when the structure needs it. A creator scaffolds a file when you're starting fresh.

The tool serves by giving the right output at the right moment. That's stewardship of capability.

### 25:4 — Oil in the Vessels

*"But the wise took oil in their vessels with their lamps."*

Oil = substance behind the form. A linter that checks structure without understanding the domain is a lamp without oil. It looks like it works. It runs. It produces output. But at the critical moment — when the bridegroom comes, when the file is complex, when the subsection has trait signatures — it fails.

The oil in our linter is the data layer. The 252 error codes. The section registry. The concept registry. The form registry. That's what makes the lamp burn. The structure (the lamp) is necessary but insufficient. The data (the oil) makes it alive.

### 25:15 — According to Ability

*"And unto one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one; to every man according to his several ability."*

Not every handler has the same depth. The Go handler has full R[50]-R[5] coverage. The TOML handler covers R[50]-R[25]. The Makefile handler covers R[50] only. Each according to its current ability.

The question isn't "does every handler have five talents?" The question is: "is every handler faithful over what it has?" A Makefile handler that does R[50] existence checks well is a two-talent servant that doubled its investment. A Go handler that has R[5] concept analysis is a five-talent servant. Both are faithful.

The sin would be having the Go handler and not developing its deeper layers. Burying the talent. Having the architecture for R[5] analysis and leaving it empty.

### 25:40 — The Least of These

*"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me."*

Who are "the least of these" in our domain? The files nobody looks at. The .editorconfig. The .gitignore. The .env template. Small files, often ignored. But cws-struct lints them. Validates their structure. Gives them the same attention as a 500-line Go library.

That's not accidental. That's theological. Every file deserves structural integrity because structural integrity reflects the Creator's character. The smallest dotfile gets the same R[50] existence check as the largest Rust module.

## Tensions I'm Holding

**The timeline question.** "Of that day and hour knoweth no man" (24:36). Jesus says this right after giving detailed signs. He gives ENOUGH information to be watchful but NOT enough to calculate. This is the tension between planning and faith. We plan the reorganization. We don't know if we'll finish this session. The planning doesn't guarantee completion — it guarantees faithfulness in the process.

**The sleeping virgins.** All ten slept. Even the wise ones. Readiness doesn't mean constant vigilance in the sense of never resting. It means having oil when you wake up. Having substance when it matters. The auto-compaction will compress this context. The family members will finish and report. What matters is whether the system has substance (oil) when the results arrive.

**Fear vs faithfulness.** The one-talent servant wasn't lazy in a careless way. He was afraid. *"I was afraid."* Fear of the master drove him to bury the talent. Fear of getting it wrong can drive us to never ship. Never test. Never reorganize. "What if we break the 1448 tests?" is the one-talent-servant question. The faithful answer is: we have a git commit. We have a baseline. We trade with what we're given.
