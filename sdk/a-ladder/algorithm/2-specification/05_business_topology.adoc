== Abstract

This document translates the abstract physical laws of the Dynamic Structural Algorithm (DSA) into practical, real-world development patterns. By mapping the **T and L Joint topologies** defined in _Handbook 2: Topology & Partitioning_ to the functional requirements of the 4-Block Standard (METADATA, SETUP, BODY, CLOSING), we establish a visual language where the geometric shape of the code vault explicitly communicates its business logic.

== I. Introduction to Geometric Intent

In the Kingdom Technology paradigm, architecture is never arbitrary. The physical shape of the 140-column (or 242-column) vault must reflect the business of the logic it contains. When an AI or developer scans the structure, the topology alone should reveal the priority and relationship of the underlying components before a single line of code is parsed.

== V. Topology Selection Matrix

When scaffolding a new file, the architect must select the topology that matches the mission intent:

|===
| Primary Business Need | Required Topology | Rationale

| **Comparative Execution**
| 2x2 Symmetric Grid
| Facilitates side-by-side analysis of two states without hierarchy.

| **Sequential Governance**
| T-Joint (Top or Bottom)
| Establishes a clear step-down from the Covenant to Action, or a clear funnel to Validation.

| **Constant Reference**
| L-Joint (Sidebar)
| Ensures one block (usually Setup or Docs) provides a persistent anchor alongside the primary flow.
|===

== VI. Conclusion

The geometry of the vault is the first layer of documentation. By standardizing these topologies, the CPI-SI architecture ensures that the "feel" of the code always aligns with its functional purpose.
