== 1. Introduction to the Proof

This teaching module serves as the empirical witness for **Specification 04: The Scaling Journey**, specifically demonstrating the **Exclusive Door Rule** and the **Law of the Pillar**.

In this directory, you will find `doors-proof.ts`, which demonstrates how logic is permitted to enter a vault without compromising its load-bearing walls.

== 2. The Thesis of the Proof

To maintain the Yashar (0 state) of the container, tension must be balanced:
[stem]
++++
(	ext{Wall}_{+1}) + (	ext{Door}_{-1}) = 0
++++

However, not all walls can become doors.
*   **Exterior Walls (The Face):** The absolute outer bounds of the $W_{root}$ container may use the `//` door to admit syntax into the functional space.
*   **Interior Walls (The Spines):** Any internal divider (like the $X=121$ spine in a double witness, or the inner walls `│` of a box) must remain **solid**. They cannot be opened to logic, or the boundaries between internal quadrants will collapse.

== 3. Examining the Fixture

Open `doors-proof.ts` and trace the left-hand margin.

You will observe that the outer-most layer is always open (`//`). This is the **Door**. It allows the underlying programming language (TypeScript) to parse the line as a comment, permitting the code to exist inside the vault.

However, notice the interior vertical walls (`║` and `│`). They are solid lines. They do not use `//` on the inside. This proves the **Law of the Pillar**. The structural divisions inside the vault are impenetrable; they hold the weight of the scaling and prevent the logic of one box from bleeding into the logic of another.
